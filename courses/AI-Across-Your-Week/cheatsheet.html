The matching rule: match the block's verb, never its topic. A budget block can be a writing block, a presenting block, or an explaining block.

The Week Map, four steps

  1. Inventory: list the week's recurring blocks with an honest hour cost each. Use last week's real calendar; keep it roles and workflows.
  2. Match: pair each block with the method that fits its verb.
  3. Stack: chain two methods on one task (minutes into drafts, numbers into slides). Check the output of link one before it feeds link two.
  4. Bank: name where the saved time goes, block it on the calendar, and report one line on what it bought.

The traffic light (data rule)

  • Green: public or generic information. Go.
  • Yellow: internal work. Approved VU tools only.
  • Red: private information about people. Never.

The light outranks the map, whatever the time savings look like.

The three go-to stacks

  • Minutes into drafts: meeting record, checked, feeds the follow-up email.
  • Numbers into slides: checked data read feeds the deck brief.
  • Answers into a decision brief: verified research feeds the criteria and tradeoffs.

The weakest-verify law: a chain is only as trustworthy as its softest check. The check you want to skip is the one to write down.

The numbers, for the skeptics

  • Seven-plus distinct uses: roughly 2x as likely to report real productivity gains as one or two uses (Gallup and Gallup, 2026 (employees whose manager actively supports AI use are 1.7x as likely to use it weekly)).
  • The biggest group of users sits parked at one or two uses; the plateau is the norm.
  • Frequent users report gains far more often than occasional users.
  • Recovered time refills with meetings unless deliberately banked (Leadership Circle).
