Admin, automated: the cheat sheet

The Repeat Audit, four moves

  1. Spot it: anything done more than twice the same way is a candidate.
  2. Template it: the reusable skeleton with slots, built from three past examples.
  3. Route it: rules and AI triage for what arrives; you read every send.
  4. Retire it: some recurring work should simply stop. Test it safely.

Ranking rule: frequency times sameness. The more often and the more identical, the higher the payoff.

The three tells of a repeat

  • The search: you hunt for a past email to copy it.
  • The rebuild: you rebuild the same table monthly, new numbers only.
  • The echo: you type the same three sentences weekly, from memory.

The manifesto: anything you have typed three times is a template you have not met yet.

Template hygiene, three rules

  • Slots, never leftovers: every name, date, and number becomes a [BRACKET].
  • Your voice survives: a template you always rewrite is a tax with extra steps.
  • De-identified sources only: strip people's details before AI sees any example.

The cautionary tale: the leftover-name incident. Last month's name, this month's inboxes.

The traffic light (data rule)

  • Green: public or generic information. Go.
  • Yellow: internal work. Approved VU tools only.
  • Red: private information about people. Never.

The light outranks every technique on this sheet, whatever the time savings.

Three-pile triage, in order

Pile 1, needs me today: a real deadline inside the day with your name on it. Handle first. Pile 2, needs me this week: real asks without a same-day clock; AI drafts from your templates, you read, you send. Pile 3, does not need me: newsletters, confirmations, cc noise; sweep rules file these on arrival, with a weekly ten-minute scan as the net. The standing rule: rules sort, AI drafts, you decide. Nothing sends without your eyes.

Calendar rules that hold

  1. Default meetings to 25 and 50 minutes; the shaved minutes are yours.
  2. Two named focus blocks a week, defended like meetings.
  3. A saved two-sentence decline template, slots for the meeting and the alternative.

Rules decide once. Willpower has to win every day, and it loses on the busy ones.

The numbers, for the skeptics

  • Around 77% of AI users report clear productivity gains from automating repetitive tasks (Gallup).
  • Automating repetition is the highest-payoff everyday AI use case Gallup measures.
  • The catch: saved time evaporates into meetings unless deliberately banked (Leadership Circle).
