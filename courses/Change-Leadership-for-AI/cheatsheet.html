Change leadership, the cheat sheet

The method: name, frame, invite, hold

  1. Name: say the fear out loud before it says itself.
  2. Frame: honest scope: what changes, what doesn't, what you don't know yet.
  3. Invite: the team into the design; people support what they help build.
  4. Hold: revisit, re-answer, protect the strugglers, for months.

The naming scripts

"Some of you are wondering if this is how your job changes. Let's talk about that directly, starting with what I know and what I don't."

"This will change parts of how we work. I don't know all of it yet. What I can promise: any news about this team's roles, you hear from me first, with the team in the room."

The honest answer to the job question

Part one, what you know: "Tasks change first, roles later, and no role decision about this team has been made." Part two, what you promise: "Any role news comes from me first, and the hours we save go to [the real work that keeps getting skipped], where you can see them go." Never promise nothing will change; promise how change will be handled. If people suspect saved hours convert into cut roles, they will make sure there are no saved hours.

The honesty rules

  • Never promise that nothing will change; you can't keep it.
  • Do promise how change is handled: with the team, visibly.
  • Answer the saved-hours question out loud, unprompted.
  • Say "I don't know" when you don't; then say what you'll do to find out.

Safety to stumble

  • Public experiments: a named low-stakes sandbox; attempts get demoed, misses included.
  • No-blame debriefs: the process is the finding, never the person.
  • Go first: your own fumble story, told plainly, is the cheapest safety there is.
  • Watch the quiet ones: they decide whether the change sticks.

The ethics session (you host it)

  • One hour, one page out: fine / not fine / escalating / revisit date.
  • Ask before ruling; disagreement is the agenda.
  • Escalate what belongs to policy owners; never improvise policy.
  • Reopen when capabilities shift. They will.

The traffic light (the floor)

  • Green: public or generic information. Go.
  • Yellow: internal work. Approved VU tools only.
  • Red: private information about people. Never.

The light outranks everything in this course, whatever the efficiency case.

Hold for the long arc

The revisit cadence: a monthly pulse, and every decision gets a revisit date. The quiet signals: who stopped speaking in meetings, who never mentions the tool, who brings polish and never problems. Courage as a habit: the true thing, said early, including upward. Connection as the multiplier: the 1:1 fear question, asked plainly, heard without defending. The measure: trust alongside usage; a green dashboard with quiet meetings is a warning, never a win.

The numbers, for the skeptics

  • Around 18% of leaders lead AI investments effectively (Accenture).
  • The differentiators: curiosity, courage, connection, not tool fluency.
  • Around 15% of employees say leadership has communicated a clear AI plan (Gallup).
  • Under a quarter of U.S. employees strongly trust their leadership (Gallup). Your announcement lands on that baseline.

The 20-minute naming conversation

  1. Name the fear, out loud, first.
  2. Frame: changing / not changing / unknown.
  3. Answer the job question, both parts, unprompted.
  4. Invite: the team designs what gets tried first.
  5. Schedule the ethics session in the room.
