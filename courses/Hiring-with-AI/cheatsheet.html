Hiring, Human, the cheat sheet

The red side: never in any AI tool

  • Resumes, applications, references, interview notes on named candidates.
  • Anything identifying a person in a hiring process, including "just checking tone."
  • And AI never ranks, scores, or screens people. Automated employment decisions are regulated territory; the decision is yours.

The one-breath test: could a specific person be identified from this paste? If yes, it never goes in.

The green side: where AI earns its keep

  • Job descriptions: requirement audits, plain-language rewrites, jargon checks.
  • Structured interviews: behavioral questions from the role's real tasks.
  • Scoring rubrics: 1-to-5 anchors, drafted and pressure-tested, before candidates.
  • Skills maps: what the team lacks, before the posting is written.
  • Process communications: outreach and templates with nobody named in them.

The traffic light (data rule)

  • Green: public or generic information. Go.
  • Yellow: internal work. Approved VU tools only (ChatGPT EDU, Amplify, Copilot).
  • Red: private information about people. Never.

The light outranks every technique on this sheet, every time.

The before-candidates rule

Everything structural is built before the first application is read: questions, anchors, weights. Then it freezes. Structure built after you've met people bends toward the one you already like, and a rubric written before candidates contains no candidate, which keeps AI's help green.

The rubric skeleton

One row per real task the role performs, plus one for the probe (the quality you always wish you had asked about). Each row: a behavioral question ("tell me about a time...") and 1-to-5 anchors in observable evidence: what a 2 sounds like, what a 5 sounds like. The stranger test: someone who never met you could score an answer using only your words. Draft with AI in an approved tool; pressure-test by asking whether two raters would score the same answer the same way.

The requirement audit

  1. The question, per line: does this describe the work, or the person we're picturing?
  2. Proxies out: "digital native," "native English speaker," culture-coded phrasing.
  3. Degree lines defend themselves or leave; skills can do most jobs degrees claim.
  4. Plain-language pass: reading level down, insider jargon out.

The bias guardrails

  • Diverse review of AI-drafted questions and anchors before any candidate sees them.
  • The same-evidence test: identical facts get identical scrutiny.
  • Why before rank: evidence written per candidate before any ordering.
  • Anything automated touching candidates: HR and VU policy first, always.

The 30-minute structure session, fill in the blanks

Role: [the role and the team gap it fills]. Tasks: [three things it really does]. Questions: [one per task, same for every candidate]. Anchors: [a 2 sounds like... a 5 sounds like...]. Posting audit: [proxies and degree lines challenged]. Filed at: [where the hiring panel will find it]. All before a single application arrives.
