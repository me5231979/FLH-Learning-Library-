Numbers, faster: the cheat sheet

The Data Loop

  1. Describe: tell the AI what the data is: columns, period, source, quirks.
  2. Ask: one question at a time, climbing the ladder rung by rung.
  3. Check: recompute one number, check totals and counts, trace surprises to rows.
  4. Tell: two plain sentences a busy reader can act on, plus one chart.

Loop rule: the check is never optional, and the recommendation is never the AI's.

The traffic light, for data files

  • Green: public or generic data. Go.
  • Yellow: internal data with no people in it, including de-identified files. Approved VU tools only: ChatGPT EDU, Amplify, Copilot.
  • Red: private information about people: names, IDs, salaries, grades. Never in unapproved tools.

The light outranks the loop, and one name in a comment field makes the whole file red.

De-identify in five minutes

  1. Copy the file; never touch the original.
  2. Delete name, initial, ID, email, and phone columns.
  3. Read the free-text columns by eye; names hide in comments.
  4. Aggregate to counts by month or unit when counts will do.

The question ladder

  1. Summarize: what does a typical month look like?
  2. Compare: this period against last, in a scannable table.
  3. Outliers: which rows or months break the pattern?
  4. Explain: what changed, and show me the rows behind it.

The describe paragraph, fill in the blanks

"This is [what the file is], one row per [unit], covering [period], from [source system]. Columns: [name each and what it holds]. Quirks: [gaps, renames, double-counts]. First question: [rung one]."

The check ritual, every run

  1. Recompute one number: the one that will be quoted.
  2. Totals and counts: parts sum to the whole; row count matches the file.
  3. Row trace: "show me which rows that came from" on every surprise.

The tells: parts that miss the total, percentages over 100, categories the file never had, months past the data's edge.

The numbers, for the skeptics

  • About 3 in 4 employees using AI on data work report clear productivity gains (Gallup), with the widest gains among frequent users.
  • Data and analysis work ranks near the top of AI use cases by payoff, and most of it is still done by hand.
  • Workers average around 4 hours a week fixing AI output (SHRM): the cost of checking too late.

The telling: two sentences and a chart

Sentence one: the change, with its number. Sentence two: why the reader should care, or what to look at next. Chart brief: the comparison + the audience + the takeaway line under the chart. Never outsourced: the recommendation; the "so we should" sentence carries your name.
