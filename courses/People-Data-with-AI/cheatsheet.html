People Data, Safely: the cheat sheet

The Aggregate Rule (the traffic light)

  • Green: published benchmarks and generic information. Go.
  • Yellow: internal work, including aggregated, de-identified team-level data. Approved VU tools only (ChatGPT EDU, Amplify, Copilot).
  • Red: private information about people: names, individual scores, identifiable comments, your notes about a person. Never.

The light outranks every technique on this sheet, whatever the deadline.

The Coach's Loop

  1. Aggregate: get the data to team level yourself, names out, head count checked.
  2. Interrogate: ask AI for themes, contrasts, and the question behind the question.
  3. Humanize: test every theme against what you have actually seen.
  4. Converse: the output is always a conversation, never an automated verdict.

De-identify like you mean it

  • Names out, obviously.
  • Identifying roles out: "the only designer" is a name with extra steps.
  • Identifying events out: "after the March escalation" narrows the cast.
  • Head count: under 5 respondents in any slice, the slice stays red.

The small-n trap: a "team-level" average from 3 people is a disclosure with a label.

The three interrogation questions

  1. Themes: "What are the strongest themes, and quote the data that supports each one?"
  2. Contrast: "What moved since last cycle, and what stayed stuck?"
  3. The question behind the question: "What would explain both X and Y?"

Ground truth test: a theme with no example you have seen is a hypothesis, and hypotheses go to the team as questions.

The rehearsal prompt pattern (roles, never names)

"A [role] on my team [observable pattern, no names, no identifying detail]. I want to give this feedback using [SBI or feedforward]. Here is my draft. Stress-test it: where is it vague, what could be heard as blame, and what are the three hardest reasonable responses I should be ready for?" AI rehearses; you judge. Every sentence that evaluates a person comes from your evidence, in your words.

Review prep, the division of labor

  1. Evidence: notes all year; de-identified themes by role, checked against dated examples.
  2. Drafting: AI structures; you write every judgment sentence.
  3. Calibration: check the ratings as a set: one bar, recency and visibility named.
  4. Delivery: rehearse the two hardest conversations, roles not names.

The numbers, for the skeptics

  • About 70% of the variance in team engagement traces to the manager (Gallup).
  • Only about 1 in 5 employees worldwide is engaged at work (Gallup).
  • About 1 in 3 US employees now uses AI at work; only about a fifth say their org has a clear AI plan (Gallup).
  • The HR tools already assume AI-assisted managers (Culture Amp AI Coach; Lattice and 15Five close behind).

The share-back, three lines

What we heard: [the theme, in plain words]. What we will try: [one visible change with a date]. What we will not change, and why: [the honest one; it builds the most trust]. Never speculate about who said what: one hunted respondent poisons every future survey. Then measure the right thing: conversations held and experiments started, never dashboard views.
