AI 201, the cheat sheet

The window, in three habits

  • Restate, don't assume: in long chats, repeat the key instruction.
  • Ask near the end: recent tokens win the attention contest.
  • Start fresh on purpose: a clean prompt beats message 40 of a cluttered chat.
  • Why: the model re-reads a finite window every turn. Outside it, nothing exists.

Method decision, in order

  1. Can a better prompt fix it? → Prompting. Cheapest, fastest, no infrastructure.
  2. Needs current or internal facts? → Add RAG. It grounds answers in real documents; it doesn't make the model smarter.
  3. Needs a permanent behavior change at scale? → Consider fine-tuning, and budget for redoing it every model update.

The four advanced patterns

  • Few-Shot: 2–3 worked examples before the real ask.
  • Chain-of-Thought: "reason step by step before answering" for anything multi-step or numeric.
  • Template: hand it your exact output structure for anything recurring.
  • Flipped Interaction: "ask me clarifying questions first" when you don't know what you need.

Agent failure modes & fixes

  • Wrong tool call · misread input/output · infinite loop.
  • The fix is never "trust it more": scoped tools, step limits, and a human checkpoint before anything ships.
  • Checkpoints: spot-check (low stakes) · full review (external) · named approval (money, people, commitments).

Hallucination tells

  • Suspiciously specific citations you can't find.
  • Plausible numbers off by 10x.
  • Absolute rules ("any," "always") where reality has exceptions.
  • Half-true answers whose checkable half checks out.
  • And often no tell at all: verify facts, dates, numbers, names, citations at the source.

Governance tiers 🟢🟡🔴

🟢 Green: public or generic. Any approved tool.
🟡 Yellow: internal content. Company-managed tools only (Amplify, VU-signed Copilot).
🔴 Red: FERPA, HIPAA, personnel, donors, credentials, unpublished research, contracts. Named approval, or no AI tool at all.

NIST AI RMF, as four questions

Govern: who decides and who's accountable? · Map: what AI are we running, and who does it affect? · Measure: has anyone evaluated or red-teamed it? · Manage: what do we do about what we found? The first question your team can't answer is your governance gap.
