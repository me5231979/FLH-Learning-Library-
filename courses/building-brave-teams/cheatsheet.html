Brave Teams, the cheat sheet

The definition (defend it)

  • Psychological safety: the shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking (Edmondson, 1999).
  • Not niceness, safety makes the hard thing sayable.
  • Not a lower bar, the target is both dials high: hard on the work, soft on the people.
  • Not consensus, safety protects dissent; it doesn't require agreement.

Clark's ladder, with tests

  1. Inclusion: does the newest voice get invited in, by name?
  2. Learner: what happened to the last "I don't know"?
  3. Contributor: do ideas travel with the author's name attached?
  4. Challenger: when was dissent last thanked, publicly?

The working equation

  • Safety = Invitation × Response × Fallibility.
  • Invitation: ask "what am I missing?", then wait.
  • Response: what happens to bad news, in front of everyone watching.
  • Fallibility: the senior person goes first on "I was wrong."
  • Multiply, don't add: a zero anywhere zeroes the product.

The Johari moves

  • Open grows two ways, and only two:
  • Disclosure shrinks Hidden, tell them something, on your terms.
  • Feedback shrinks Blind, ask "what two words describe me?"
  • Leave Unknown alone; time and challenge shrink it.

Expanders vs. contractors

  • Expand: names first · "what am I missing?" · public credit · steel-man the dissent · thank the messenger.
  • Contract: interrupting · "as we all know…" · eye-rolls · credit-taking · HiPPO decisions · shooting the messenger.
  • The audience, not the target, is where safety is won or lost.

The evidence, in one breath

  • Hospitals: better units report MORE errors, they hide less (Edmondson, 1996).
  • Tenerife 1977: doubt voiced too gently; 583 people. CRM exists so it's said plainly.
  • Project Aristotle: across 180+ Google teams, safety was the #1 predictor, above talent.

SBI + Ask, fill in the blanks

Situation: "In [specific meeting/moment]…" → Behavior: "…when you [what a camera saw, no verdicts, no 'always']…" → Impact: "…what I noticed/felt was [owned with I]…" → Ask: "…can we talk about [genuine question]?" Receiving? Clarifying questions only. The rebuttal forming in your head is the data.

Your commitment card, fill in the blanks
