Coaching, the cheat sheet

GROW: the question bank

  1. Goal: "What would make this conversation useful?" · "What does success look like?"
  2. Reality: "What's actually happening?" · "What have you tried?" · "Where are things, 1–10?"
  3. Options: "What could you do?" · "What else?" (three times) · "If nothing stood in the way?"
  4. Will: "What will you do, by when?" · "What might get in the way?" · "Committed, 1–10?" (under 8 = keep coaching)

Powerful questions: 3 tests

  • Open: can't be answered yes or no.
  • Clean: doesn't smuggle your answer in ("have you tried…", "don't you think…").
  • Theirs: aimed at their thinking, not your curiosity.
  • Then wait: count six seconds. Whoever breaks the silence first buys the problem.
  • The all-purpose one: "What else?"

Feedforward (Goldsmith)

  • 1. Name one behavior you want to improve.
  • 2. Ask two people for two suggestions each, future only.
  • 3. Respond only with "thank you." No explaining, no defending.
  • One-liner version: "I'm working on interrupting less. Two ideas for next week?"

Which model, when

  • GROW: everyday coaching conversations. The default.
  • CLEAR: ongoing coaching relationships; contract expectations up front, review at the end.
  • OSKAR: coachees stuck in problem-analysis; scale it ("you're at a 4; what's already working?").

Your opener, ready to say

"What would make this conversation most useful to you?"  ·  Then listen more than you talk, write your advice down instead of saying it, and end with: "So what will you do, by when? And 1 to 10, how committed are you?"

Coach vs. direct

  • Direct when: emergency, compliance, brand-new person, a decision you own.
  • Coach when: they could solve it and grow, the problem keeps returning, multiple right answers, development talk.
  • Default: coaching. Most managers have the ratio backwards.

The telling traps

  • The answer itch: write it down instead of saying it.
  • Advice with a question mark: if your question contains your solution, re-aim it.
  • Silence panic: six seconds. The discomfort is them thinking.
  • Remember: a manager's answer solves today; a manager's question builds tomorrow.
