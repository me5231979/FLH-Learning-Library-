Leading the Shift: the cheat sheet

The method: show, say, set, sustain

  1. Show: use AI visibly, misses included. Narrate the prompt, the draft, and the fix.
  2. Say: explicit permission and explicit limits: the encouraged list, the never list, who to ask.
  3. Set: norms in the rituals you already run: the 1:1 question, the share-one round, the miss log.
  4. Sustain: protect learning time, celebrate caught errors, keep it on the agenda past month three.

The 90-second narration

Four sentences, in the team meeting: the task ("the quarterly update"), the prompt ("here's what I asked and why"), the fix ("the draft got these two things wrong; I corrected them against the tracker"), the check ("I verified it against the source, never against how it read"). Real and slightly imperfect beats polished; the miss is the credibility.

The 1:1 question

"What did you try with AI this week, and what did it get wrong?" Two minutes, every week, for a quarter. The second half assumes misses exist, which makes reporting them normal. Answers tell you who's experimenting, who's stuck, and who's still waiting for permission.

The permission talk, outline

Opener: "I want to be explicit about AI on this team, because I'd rather you hear it from me than guess." Encouraged: [2 to 3 named uses, each with its check]. Never: [the hard limits, starting from the traffic light's red]. Gray zone: [the unclear case you know is coming, answered], plus who to ask next time, and the promise that asking is applauded. Repeat in a month, and for every new hire. A norm heard once is a rumor.

The traffic light (data rule)

  • Green: public or generic information. Go.
  • Yellow: internal work. Approved VU tools only (ChatGPT EDU, Amplify, Copilot).
  • Red: private information about people. Never.

The light outranks every technique in this course, whatever the efficiency case.

The numbers, for the skeptics

  • ~40% of U.S. employees use AI at work; 19% use it frequently (Gallup).
  • Only 22% say their organization has communicated a clear AI plan.
  • Clear leadership plan: employees are 4.7x as likely to feel comfortable using AI.
  • The two predictors that matter: visible manager use and real-workflow integration.

The team rituals

  • Share-one round: two minutes, weekly, rotated through everyone; misses count as shares.
  • Miss log: no names on the miss, full credit on the catch, reviewed monthly.
  • The pairing move: enthusiast plus skeptic on one real task; doubt becomes verification.
  • Install one at a time, four weeks apart. Go first on all of them.

Month three, the playbook

  • The dip is normal. Calendar a week-ten note: "the round will be quiet; seed it."
  • Defend the learning hour like a client meeting; a perk dies, a budget survives.
  • Celebrate catches publicly; the miss log is proof the checks work.
  • Report stories, team-level: uses shared, misses caught, hours returned. Never rank individuals.
