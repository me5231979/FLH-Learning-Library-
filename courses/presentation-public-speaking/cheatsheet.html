Presenting, the cheat sheet

The Big Idea (Duarte) — write this before anything else

[Your point of view, a stance someone could oppose] + because + [what's at stake, ideally with a number]. One sentence, one breath, one idea. "Our Q3 results" is a topic. "Double down on enterprise — because SMB churn just outpaced new-logo growth" is a Big Idea.

Sparkline (persuasion / change)

  1. What Is: today's pain, concrete enough to sting.
  2. What Could Be: the vision.
  3. What Is: the honest obstacle (builds credibility).
  4. What Could Be: bigger vision, higher stakes.
  5. New Bliss: the world after they say yes. End here, never on an obstacle.

SCQA (executive room / decision)

  1. Situation: facts the room already accepts. Shortest part.
  2. Complication: what makes now untenable — deadline + named stakes.
  3. Question: the exact question your answer answers.
  4. Answer: stated FIRST for executives: specific, costed, dated. Support top-down, MECE.

Which structure? The 10-second audience test

Decision needed, senior room, scarce time → SCQA (lead with the answer). Hearts and minds, change story, keynote, all-hands → Sparkline (build the gap, end on New Bliss). If an executive has given you ten minutes, suspense is a liability.

Delivery (Anderson · Gallo · AMA)

  • Conversational, not oratorical — match the register to the room.
  • No bullet walls: one idea per slide; detail goes to the handout.
  • Modulate: the voice is the highlighter; pause before the ask.
  • Never over-apologize: no one cares about your mistake but you.
  • Rehearse out loud, timed, once with one honest human. Reading is not rehearsal.

The tough-question bridge (AMA)

  1. Acknowledge the question honestly (concede what's true in it).
  2. Answer directly — or commit to a date: "I'll have it to you Thursday."
  3. Return to the Big Idea: "and the reason this matters for today's decision is…"

Prep: write your three most-feared questions and one-sentence answers before you present.

The numbers, for the skeptics

  • Public speaking: 59th of 85 fears (Chapman 2024). The fear is folklore.
  • Gravitas: 67% of senior execs call it the core leadership signal; communication 28% (Coqual/Hewlett).
  • Executive presence ≈ 26% of what it takes to get promoted.
  • Communication skills: the #1 quality (39%) CFOs want in the next CFO (Deloitte).

Before you build: SHRM's six questions

  • Who's the audience?
  • What's the goal?
  • What are the messages?
  • What's the content?
  • Where's the feedback?
  • How do I refine it?
