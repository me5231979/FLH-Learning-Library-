Start Smarter, the cheat sheet

What is AI, in one sentence

A computer program that learned from millions of examples, so it can predict, suggest, and write things that fit your situation. Not a robot, not magic, not always right. You already use it: word suggestions, spam filters, map routes.

How to use it (the whole skill)

  1. Ask in plain English, like texting a capable assistant. Details help: who it's for, what it's about, how it should sound.
  2. Look at the answer. It's a draft, not a verdict.
  3. Improve: "shorter," "warmer," "add the deadline." One follow-up is where it gets good.

The traffic light: ask it before you paste 🟢🟡🔴

🟢 Green: go ahead: public topics, generic drafts, brainstorming, "explain this." Any approved tool.
🟡 Yellow: approved VU tools only: internal work content (your drafts, work docs). Copilot signed in with your VU account: never a random free website.
🔴 Red: keep it out: private info about real people: names with grades, health, personnel issues, passwords. Describe it without names, or don't use AI for it.

The five superpowers

  • Draft it: first versions of emails, notes, letters.
  • Summarize it: long document → the five things you need.
  • Brainstorm it: ten ideas in ten seconds; you pick.
  • Explain it: jargon → plain English, no embarrassment.
  • Rewrite it: same facts, different tone or length.

The two rules that keep you safe

  • Check what matters. AI sounds confident even when wrong. Facts, dates, numbers, names: verify before your name goes on them.
  • You do the judgment. AI does chores; decisions about people stay with people. It doesn't get a vote.

Why it's worth it

Workers who use these tools report saving about 2.2 hours in a 40-hour week (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, 2024 survey). The same technology helps doctors find 29% more cancers in screening (The Lancet Digital Health, MASAI trial), describes the world for blind users (Be My Eyes), and rebuilds voices for people with ALS (Bridging Voice). AI does the chore; a person stays in charge. Every time.

Your starter prompt, fill in the blanks

[The job: draft / summarize / give me ideas / explain] + [your details: who it's for, what it's about, how it should sound] + [the shape: short / bullets / step by step]. Example: "Write a warm, three-sentence email to my six-person team inviting them to Thursday's planning lunch. Keep it short."

Then the two-step: read the answer, add one detail it was missing, ask again.
