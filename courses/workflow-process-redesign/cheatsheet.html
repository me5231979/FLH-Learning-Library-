Workflow redesign, the cheat sheet

The four step types

  1. Gather: collect the inputs. High volume, low judgment, eats the hours.
  2. Transform: shape the first version: the update, the summary, the deck.
  3. Judge: check it against sources and standards. Is it right, good, safe?
  4. Commit: send, approve, spend, hire. Where accountability lives.

Map rule: one real instance, five to nine steps, interview the person who actually does it.

The pattern: draft, verify, decide

  1. AI drafts: gather and transform steps with catchable errors.
  2. A human verifies: a named owner checks against real sources, with a reject path.
  3. A human decides: judge and commit steps. Accountability doesn't delegate.

Assign once, per workflow, at design time. Assignments attach to steps, never to people.

The verifier's rubric

  • Named owner: the check is in someone's job, in writing.
  • Source-checked: against the tracker, the data, the notes. Never against "sounds right."
  • Reject path plus a log: misses get written down; catch rate becomes a metric.
  • The warning light: a check that has never caught anything means nobody's looking.

The traffic light (data rule)

  • Green: public or generic information. Go.
  • Yellow: internal work. Approved VU tools only.
  • Red: private information about people. Never.

The light outranks the pattern, whatever the efficiency case.

The sorting questions, for any step

Volume or judgment? High-volume mechanical work drafts freely; judgment keeps its human. The error question: if this step is wrong, who gets hurt, and how fast would we find out? Fast-caught and cheap: draft it. Slow and expensive: keep the human earlier. The budget test: if the check costs what the work did, the step was never a draft candidate.

The pilot standard

  1. One workflow, two to four weeks.
  2. The doers design the checks.
  3. A visible metric: hours saved AND errors caught.
  4. A real rollback, available without shame.
  5. An honest, out-loud answer to what the saved hours are for.

The numbers, for the skeptics

  • 88% of organizations use AI; around 6% get real bottom-line value (McKinsey, State of AI).
  • Workflow redesign: the strongest measured link to EBIT impact from gen AI.
  • High performers are 2.8x more likely to have fundamentally redesigned workflows (55% vs 20%).
  • Only about 1 in 5 gen AI users have redesigned any workflow at all.
