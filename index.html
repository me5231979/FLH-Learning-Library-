Part of the Manager Voyage program

Grows self and othersMakes effective and ethical decisions for the UniversityPeople AnalyticsCoaching TechniquesAI Literacy

Coaching for Performance taught the human method. This teaches the data side, inside hard privacy lines. Culture Amp, Lattice, and 15Five are all pushing managers toward AI-assisted people insight, and the skill that matters is doing it without ever putting a person into a tool. The spine is the Aggregate Rule: individuals are red, always; aggregated and de-identified team data is yellow, approved VU tools only. On top of it sits the Coach's Loop, ending where people data should always end, in a conversation.

  • 6 sections · 4 trainers, a private interrogation planner, and the graded Review Prep Lab
  • Classroom 90 min (60 core) · self-paced ~45 min
  • In person, virtual, or self-paced
Full course description (printable)
Learning objectives
  1. Apply the Aggregate Rule to every people-data case before any of it goes near an AI tool
  2. Use AI to find themes in engagement results and your own meeting notes, at team level with names removed
  3. Draft hard feedback with AI as a rehearsal partner while the judgment stays yours, in your words
  4. Prepare a review cycle where AI organizes your evidence and you write every verdict
  5. Run the follow-up conversation the data was collected for, and measure that it actually happened

Making It Normal

Classroom 90 min · Self-paced ~45 min

Part of the Manager Voyage program

Leads and inspires teamsRadically collaborates and cultivates belongingChange ManagementAI LiteracyTeam Development

The 90-minute companion to the short Leading the Shift course, for managers who want the full method. Gallup names manager modeling and workflow integration the two strongest predictors of a team actually using AI, and most employees say nobody ever told them what is encouraged. This session teaches Show, Say, Set, Sustain: narrating your own use including the misses, giving explicit permission and explicit limits, installing AI into the rituals where habits live, and holding the practice past the month-three novelty cliff.

  • 6 sections · 4 trainers, a private permission-talk builder, and the graded Adoption Lab
  • Classroom 90 min (60 core) · self-paced ~45 min
  • In person, virtual, or self-paced
Full course description (printable)
Learning objectives
  1. Explain why a manager's visible AI use predicts team adoption better than mandates, licenses, or memos
  2. Model your own AI use out loud, including the checks you ran and the misses you caught
  3. Give your team explicit permission and explicit limits, with the traffic light as the hard edge
  4. Install AI norms into the 1:1s and team meetings you already run, without adding a single meeting
  5. Sustain adoption past the novelty cliff with protected learning time and metrics that tell stories

Delegating, Rethought

Classroom 90 min · Self-paced ~45 min

Part of the Manager Voyage program

Grows self and othersLeads and inspires teamsDelegationRole DesignAI Literacy

The same task can now go to AI, to a junior as a stretch assignment, or stay with you, and nobody trained managers for that choice. Route everything routine to the machine and you hollow out the apprenticeship your team learns by. Stanford GSB frames the shift as leaders redesigning roles rather than overseeing execution. This session teaches the Routing Test (growth first, then judgment, then volume) and the role math for rebuilding a job as AI absorbs its routine layer.

  • 6 sections · 4 trainers, a private role-math sketch, and the graded Routing Lab
  • Classroom 90 min (60 core) · self-paced ~45 min
  • In person, virtual, or self-paced
Full course description (printable)
Learning objectives
  1. Apply the Routing Test's three questions, growth, judgment, volume, to the real tasks crossing your desk
  2. Distinguish stretch work from grunt work before you route either one
  3. Protect the apprenticeship pipeline as AI absorbs the tasks juniors used to learn by
  4. Redesign a role around what grows, judgment reps, client contact, verification ownership, instead of just shrinking its task list
  5. Brief a delegation, to a person or to AI, so it lands with a clear outcome and named checks

Hiring, Human

Classroom 90 min · Self-paced ~45 min

Part of the Manager Voyage program

Makes effective and ethical decisions for the UniversityGrows self and othersStructured InterviewingTalent ManagementResponsible AI

Pasting a resume into a chatbot feels efficient and is a violation: candidate materials are private information about people, and AI never ranks, screens, or scores a human being. This session draws that line hard, then teaches everything valuable on the right side of it. Structure is the most evidence-backed fairness tool in hiring, and AI is excellent at building structure: inclusive postings, requirement audits that catch proxies, behavioral questions, and scoring anchors written before a single application is read.

  • 6 sections · 4 trainers, a private structure planner, and the graded Hiring Lab
  • Classroom 90 min (60 core) · self-paced ~45 min
  • In person, virtual, or self-paced
Full course description (printable)
Learning objectives
  1. Classify the materials in a hiring process with the traffic light and defend the red line, including the cases that feel harmless
  2. Use AI to draft job descriptions and structured interviews that widen the pool instead of quietly shrinking it
  3. Build a scoring rubric before any application is read, with anchors a stranger could apply
  4. Run a skills map of your team so the next posting names what the team actually lacks
  5. Recognize where bias enters AI-assisted hiring and apply the guardrails that catch it

Change That Sticks

Classroom 90 min · Self-paced ~45 min

Part of the Manager Voyage program

Leads and inspires teamsDevelops and implements University strategyChange ManagementPsychological SafetyCommunication

For managers announcing an AI change their team did not ask for. AI change fails on human ground: announced as technology, experienced as threat. Accenture's leadership research finds the effective minority differ in curiosity, courage, and connection rather than tool fluency. This session teaches Name, Frame, Invite, Hold, the course's working method: saying the fear out loud before it says itself, answering the job-security question honestly without promising what you cannot, inviting the team into the design, and holding the container across months.

  • 6 sections · 4 trainers, a private conversation planner, and the graded Change Lab
  • Classroom 90 min (60 core) · self-paced ~45 min
  • In person, virtual, or self-paced
Full course description (printable)
Learning objectives
  1. Explain why AI change fails on human ground rather than technical ground, and what the effective 18 percent do differently
  2. Name the fear and the job-security question directly, without false promises, using the honesty rules
  3. Build psychological safety around experimenting with AI: public experiments, no-blame misses, and going first
  4. Facilitate your team's own ethics and governance conversation instead of outsourcing it to a policy document
  5. Sustain change leadership over months: the revisit cadence, the quiet signals, and measuring trust alongside usage

AI 201: Beyond the Basics

Classroom 90 min · Self-paced ~45 min

Embodies an entrepreneurial spirit and leverages data and technologyMakes effective and ethical decisions for the UniversityArtificial IntelligenceData GovernanceRisk Management

You can prompt. This one opens the hood. Why a chatbot loses the thread of a long conversation, why it invents a citation one day and quotes the real policy the next, and who is watching once a tool starts acting on its own. Covers context and attention, grounding with RAG, choosing between prompting, RAG, and fine-tuning, four advanced prompt patterns, the agent loop and its failure modes, and the governance tiers that keep it all safe.

  • 7 sections · a hallucination-spotting lab, a knowledge check, and a governed workflow you build and keep
  • Classroom 90 min · self-paced ~45 min
  • In person, virtual, or self-paced
Full course description (printable)
Learning objectives
  1. Explain how context and attention shape an answer, and why long conversations lose the thread
  2. Describe what Retrieval-Augmented Generation fixes, and what it doesn’t
  3. Choose between prompting, RAG, and fine-tuning for a task, weighing cost, data, and maintenance
  4. Apply four advanced prompt patterns: Few-Shot, Chain-of-Thought, Template, and Flipped Interaction
  5. Recognize the agent loop, name its documented failure modes, and place the human checkpoints
  6. Classify information into governance tiers and build one real, governed AI workflow for your own work
