# AI-enabled Education Series house style

Every course in the AI-enabled Education Series follows these rules. The goal: copy that
sounds like a person at Vanderbilt wrote it, because one did (or approved it).
Run this checklist on every page before a course ships.

## The reader's three questions

Every page must answer, in plain language, before anything clever happens:

1. **What is this?** What am I looking at and what am I being asked to do?
2. **Why do I care?** What's in it for me, concretely, at my job?
3. **What do I do with it?** The specific next action, ideally tomorrow morning.

If a page can't answer all three, the page isn't done.

## Banned patterns (the AI tells)

- **No em dashes.** Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead.
- **No aphorism headlines.** "The blank page is a chore. Move it." is out.
  Say the plain thing: "Why this is worth your time."
- **No staccato drama.** One short emphatic sentence is fine. A run of
  clipped fragments ("Three moves. Every time. No excuses.") is out.
- **No "not just X, it's Y"** constructions.
- **No rule-of-three padding.** Two examples or four are allowed to exist.
- **No -ing add-on clauses** that fake depth ("...highlighting the importance
  of readiness").
- **No AI vocabulary:** delve, crucial, pivotal, showcase, landscape,
  tapestry, testament, underscore, foster, vibrant, seamless, robust.
- **No generic upbeat closers** ("Exciting times ahead!"). End on the next
  action instead.
- **Complete sentences over fragments.** Write like you talk, not like a
  slide deck.

## Required habits

- **Unnamed people in scenarios are they/them.** A director, a vendor, a
  colleague in an example gets no assumed gender. Rewrite to avoid the
  pronoun entirely when it reads better.
- **No dead-end placeholders.** If an exercise says "paste the report" or
  "fill in the blanks," the course must supply a way to actually do it:
  a practice document, a typable field, or a named public source.

- **Specifics beat abstractions.** A real building, a real Tuesday, a real
  email someone dreaded. When drafting a scenario, ask the program owner for
  the real version before inventing one.
- **Second person, active voice.** "You'll write the brief" not "the brief
  will be written."
- **Every statistic is cited.** Numbered superscript to a full Sources page.
  Peer-reviewed or top-tier research org only. No invented numbers, ever.
- **Read it aloud before shipping.** If a sentence is hard to say, rewrite
  it. Have one colleague outside the project read a page and flag anything
  that "sounds like a robot."
- **Voice source of truth:** copy approved by the program owner (Matt) sets
  the voice. When in doubt, match how he'd say it out loud, not how a
  brochure would print it.

## Program conventions

- Courses are numbered ("Course 02") and carry the line
  "Part of the AI-enabled Education Series." AI-enabled Education Series is the
  program (the ecosystem every AI course and activity belongs to); course
  NAMES stay soft and never contain the word "AI" (Working Smarter, First
  Drafts Faster); course content may say AI freely.
- Tool guidance is consistent everywhere: ChatGPT EDU for everyday
  non-sensitive work; anything sensitive or internal goes in Amplify or
  Copilot through a Vanderbilt account; when in doubt, Amplify.
- CRIT (Context, Role, Interview, Task) is credited to Geoff Woods,
  *The AI-Driven Leader*, with a link to the Working Smarter course.
- Every course ships as two separate editions at two separate links: a
  classroom edition (QR welcome, group activities, discussion-notes
  takeaway) and a self-paced edition (reflection prompts, commitment
  takeaway). No mode picker; each edition carries only its own
  experience, cross-linked in the footer. One adult-learning moment per
  major page, a printable one-page takeaway, and a full Sources/Appendix
  page in both.
