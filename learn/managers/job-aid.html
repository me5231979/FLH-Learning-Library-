Rule 1 · Whose lane is it?

The three lanes

1 · Handle itCoaching, performance, everyday friction. Most of the job lives here. Escalating it trains your team to route around you.
2 · Partner with your ECPatterns that persist after coaching. Anything touching pay, leave, or an accommodation request. Counteroffers. You stay involved; you stop deciding alone.
3 · Escalate nowAnything touching a protected class, harassment, safety, wage violations, or retaliation. Even when the person asks you to keep it quiet. Same day, every time.
Rule 2 · The first thirty seconds

When someone brings you something hard

  1. Thank them. What they just did took courage, and your face is teaching the whole team whether to speak up.
  2. Listen without verdicts. No defending, no diagnosing, no promising outcomes.
  3. Promise discretion, never secrecy. See the script below.
  4. Route it the same day using the three lanes.
Say this“I’ll be discreet, and I’ll only involve people who genuinely need to know. But if it’s something serious I’m required to act on, I have to. I’d rather be honest about that up front.”

Rule 4 · The protective sentences

Words that protect people, and the university

A health or family event surfaces“You may be entitled to job-protected leave. Let me connect you with Leave Administration today.” Then stop: no medical questions, no promises about timing or pay.
Someone needs a change to how they workTreat it as an accommodation request even if they never say “ADA.” Take it seriously, skip the diagnosis questions, loop in PCB the same day to start the interactive process.
A disclosure touches sexual misconductYou are a responsible employee. Report to the Title IX office the same day. You do not investigate, interview witnesses, or confront anyone.

Never: promise secrecy · collect medical details · approve off-the-books comp time · retaliate, or let anyone else · wait to write the note.
