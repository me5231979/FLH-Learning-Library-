Admin, Automated

Personal automation and getting repetitive work off your plate

AudienceAll Vanderbilt staff whose week is full of email, calendars, and recurring reports
LengthClassroom: 90 minutes (60-minute core path). Self-paced web edition: Core about 35 minutes, Full about 45 minutes
FormatInstructor-led, in person or virtual; learners join on their own devices via QR code. Private by design: exercises use roles and workflows, never names. Typed work stays on the learner’s screen (privacy note)
Group size8 to 24 works best for the pair drills; scales larger with room votes

About this course

Gallup finds automating repetitive tasks the single highest-payoff AI use case, with around 77 percent of users reporting clear productivity gains. It is also the use case where the gains most reliably vanish, because saved time that nobody named gets absorbed into more meetings. This session runs the Repeat Audit: spot the work you have done the same way more than twice, template it with real slots instead of last month's leftovers, route what arrives using a three-pile triage you stay accountable for, and retire the recurring obligations that should simply stop. Email triage, calendar rules, and the monthly report cycle all get worked directly. The session closes by banking the recovered hours on purpose, because a gain nobody named is a gain nobody keeps.

Learning objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  1. Identify the repetitive layer inside a real working week
  2. Build reusable templates with AI for recurring messages and reports
  3. Set up AI-assisted email triage that you remain accountable for
  4. Apply calendar rules that defend focus time
  5. Decide which recurring work to retire entirely, and bank the hours you recover

Topics covered

  • The repeat tax. How much of an ordinary week is repetition rather than work, and what the research says about automating it
  • Spot it. The recurrence inventory and its tells: searching for an old email to copy, rebuilding the same table monthly
  • Template it. Building the skeleton from de-identified examples, keeping your voice, and template hygiene so no name is ever left behind
  • Route it. The three-pile triage, AI-drafted routine replies you read before sending, and calendar rules including the decline template
  • The Automation Lab. A graded rebuild of the monthly report cycle, from gathering inputs to the send
  • Retire it and bank it. Testing retirement safely with your manager in the loop, then naming where the recovered hours go

How you'll learn

Four scenario trainers (a research guessing game, a judge-the-template drill, an inbox triage drill, and a keep-shrink-or-retire sort), a private repeat inventory that ranks a learner's own automation candidates, the graded Automation Lab with rerun, inline knowledge checks, a scored recap, and the My Repeat Audit Card capstone. Exercises run on the learner’s own screen and are not collected; see the privacy note.

Outcomes and how learning is measured

Learners leave with a dated card naming one repetitive task, the automation move they will make, the failure mode they are avoiding, and an explicit destination for the time they recover. Learning is measured in session through trainer scores, the graded lab, and a scored recap; at close through a confidence check and a spoken commitment; and afterward through a 7-day pulse on what was automated and where the hours went.

Built on Bloom's-verb objectives and instrumented with Kirkpatrick Levels 1 and 2 in session (a fist-to-five confidence check at close is Level 1; the scored recap and per-section validation checks are Level 2), with the capstone commitment card plus a 7-day pulse and a 30-day re-poll serving as Level 3 indicators; they are self-reported, so they signal transfer rather than prove it. The facilitator edition follows an ATD runbook structure (Say, Do, Ask, Debrief, Transition) with Full 90 and Core 60 timed paths. Data rules follow Vanderbilt's approved-tool guidance and the shared traffic light: green for public or generic, yellow for internal work in approved VU tools only (ChatGPT EDU, Amplify, Copilot), red for private information about people, never.
