About this course

Gallup's most striking AI finding is about breadth rather than depth: people using AI across seven or more distinct tasks are roughly twice as likely to report real productivity gains as people using it for one or two. Every other course in the series is an excellent single-use-case builder, and this one is the capstone that strings them together. Learners inventory their actual week into recurring blocks, match each block to a method from this series by its verb rather than its topic, stack two methods on a single task so the output of one becomes the input of the next, and then confront the finding that decides whether any of it lasts: saved time evaporates into more meetings unless it is deliberately banked. The graded Week Lab runs Monday to Friday for a program coordinator, and every choice in it is one the learner will make on their own calendar.

Learning objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  1. Explain why breadth of use predicts productivity gains better than intensity
  2. Inventory a real week into blocks that can be matched to AI methods
  3. Match each block to the right method from this series by its verb
  4. Stack two methods on a single task while keeping each link's check
  5. Commit to a five-assist week with the reclaimed time visibly banked

Topics covered

  • The breadth effect. Why the one-trick user plateaus, and what separates them from the seven-plus user
  • The inventory. Seeing a week as blocks, costing each one honestly, and keeping the inventory to roles and workflows
  • The method match. The series toolbox on one page, matched by verb: writing, questioning, deciding, condensing, analyzing
  • The stack. Chaining methods so one output feeds the next, and why a chain is only as trustworthy as its weakest check
  • The Week Lab. A graded Monday-to-Friday plan with outcomes at week four
  • Bank the time. Naming the destination for reclaimed hours before they disappear into the calendar

How you'll learn

Four scenario trainers (a research guessing game, a match-the-method drill, a build-the-chain drill, and a judge-the-bank drill), a private week inventory that surfaces a learner's own redesign candidates, the graded Week Lab with rerun, inline knowledge checks, a scored recap, and the My Week Map capstone. Exercises run on the learner’s own screen and are not collected; see the privacy note.

Outcomes and how learning is measured

Learners leave with a Week Map naming five blocks, the first method stack they will run, the failure mode they are avoiding, and a named destination for the hours they reclaim. Learning is measured in session through trainer scores, the graded lab, and a recap mapped to the objectives; at close through a confidence check and a spoken commitment; and afterward through a 7-day pulse counting assists actually run and a 30-day re-poll on where the time went.

Built on Bloom's-verb objectives and instrumented with Kirkpatrick Levels 1 and 2 in session (a fist-to-five confidence check at close is Level 1; the scored recap and per-section validation checks are Level 2), with the capstone commitment card plus a 7-day pulse and a 30-day re-poll serving as Level 3 indicators; they are self-reported, so they signal transfer rather than prove it. The facilitator edition follows an ATD runbook structure (Say, Do, Ask, Debrief, Transition) with Full 90 and Core 60 timed paths. Data rules follow Vanderbilt's approved-tool guidance and the shared traffic light: green for public or generic, yellow for internal work in approved VU tools only (ChatGPT EDU, Amplify, Copilot), red for private information about people, never.
