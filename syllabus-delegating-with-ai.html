Delegating, Rethought

Routing work without hollowing out your team

About this course

The same task can now go to AI, to a junior team member as a stretch assignment, or stay with you, and nobody trained managers for that choice. Route everything routine to the machine and you hollow out the apprenticeship your juniors learn by, since yesterday's growth work (first drafts, research passes, the summary nobody enjoyed) is exactly today's AI lane. Route nothing and you spend your team's hours on transcription. Stanford GSB frames the shift as leaders needing to redesign roles and processes rather than simply oversee execution. This session teaches the Routing Test in a deliberate order, growth first, then judgment, then volume, because the growth question is the one AI-era managers forget. It then teaches role math: rebuilding a job around judgment reps, verification ownership, and human contact as the routine layer drains away.

Learning objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  1. Apply the Routing Test to real tasks: the growth question, the judgment question, the volume question
  2. Distinguish stretch work from grunt work before routing either
  3. Keep the apprenticeship pipeline alive as AI absorbs routine tasks
  4. Redesign a role rather than just a task list
  5. Brief a delegation, to a person or to AI, so it lands with clear checks

Topics covered

  • The new triage. Three destinations, and why default-to-AI and default-to-people both fail
  • The Routing Test. The three questions in order, with the tells that separate them
  • Stretch versus grunt. The distinction that decides careers, and the honest problem of building new stretch when the old kind is automated
  • Role math. What backfills a role as routine drains, and how to have that conversation with the person in it
  • The Routing Lab. A graded quarter: four tasks arrive at once and every routing has consequences
  • The handoff. Briefing a delegation both ways, and why a delegation to AI is really a delegation to the verifier

How you'll learn

Four scenario trainers (a research guessing game, a route-the-task drill, a stretch-or-grunt drill, and a judge-the-handoff drill), a private role-math sketch for a real role on the learner's team, the graded Routing Lab with rerun, inline knowledge checks, a scored recap, and the My Routing Card capstone. Every section pairs teaching with something to do; nothing is watch-only. Private exercises stay on the learner's screen and are never collected.

Outcomes and how learning is measured

Learners leave with a dated card naming a task they have been holding, its destination, the handoff brief, and the growth they are protecting. Learning is measured in session through trainer scores, the graded lab, and a scored recap; at close through a confidence check and a spoken commitment; and afterward through a 7-day pulse on routings actually made and what the receiving person learned.

Built on Bloom's-verb objectives and instrumented with Kirkpatrick levels 1 to 3: a fist-to-five confidence check at close, a scored recap and per-section validation checks in session, and a capstone commitment card followed by a 7-day pulse and a 30-day re-poll. The facilitator edition follows an ATD runbook structure (Say, Do, Ask, Debrief, Transition) with Full 90 and Core 60 timed paths. Data rules follow Vanderbilt's approved-tool guidance and the shared traffic light: green for public or generic, yellow for internal work in approved VU tools only (ChatGPT EDU, Amplify, Copilot), red for private information about people, never.
