Hiring, Human

Fair, useful, and inside the lines

About this course

Pasting a resume into a chatbot feels efficient and is a violation: candidate materials are private information about people, and AI never ranks, screens, or scores a human being. This session draws that line hard and then teaches everything genuinely valuable on the right side of it, which turns out to be most of the value anyway. Structure is the most evidence-backed fairness tool in hiring, and AI is excellent at building structure and terrible at judging people. Learners use it to audit job postings for proxy requirements that quietly shrink the pool, generate behavioral questions from a role's real tasks, draft scoring anchors before a single application is read, and map the skills a team actually lacks. A full section covers where bias enters even the legitimate uses, including culture fit as a laundromat for preference, and when HR and university policy must be involved.

Learning objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  1. Classify hiring materials with the traffic light and defend the red line
  2. Use AI to draft inclusive job descriptions and audit requirements for proxies
  3. Build a scoring rubric with anchors before seeing any candidates
  4. Run a skills map for your team's real gaps
  5. Recognize where bias enters AI-assisted hiring and apply the guardrails that catch it

Topics covered

  • The temptation and the line. Why the obvious use is the forbidden one, across privacy, fairness, and regulated employment decisions
  • The red side, exactly. The full never list, including reference summaries, interview notes, and the rejection email that names a person
  • The wide funnel. Auditing postings for proxy requirements, degree inflation, and jargon, plus skills mapping before the post is written
  • Structure before candidates. The structure dividend, behavioral questions from real tasks, and anchors written before applications arrive
  • The Hiring Lab. A graded search from posting to decision, with the privacy violation among the possible outcomes
  • Bias, watched. Where bias enters legitimate uses, the same-evidence test, and when HR must be in the room

How you'll learn

Four scenario trainers (a research guessing game, an over-the-line classification drill, a fix-the-posting drill, and a spot-the-bias-door drill), a private structure planner producing question seeds and a rubric skeleton, the graded Hiring Lab with rerun, inline knowledge checks, a scored recap, and the My Hiring Structure Card capstone. Exercises run on the learner’s own screen and are not collected; see the privacy note.

Outcomes and how learning is measured

Learners leave with a dated card naming the role or talent decision in front of them, the structure they will build before seeing candidates, and an explicit red-line pledge. Learning is measured in session through trainer scores, the graded lab, and a recap mapped to the objectives; at close through a confidence check and a spoken commitment; and afterward through a 7-day pulse on rubrics built before applications were read.

Built on Bloom's-verb objectives and instrumented with Kirkpatrick Levels 1 and 2 in session (a fist-to-five confidence check at close is Level 1; the scored recap and per-section validation checks are Level 2), with the capstone commitment card plus a 7-day pulse and a 30-day re-poll serving as Level 3 indicators; they are self-reported, so they signal transfer rather than prove it. The facilitator edition follows an ATD runbook structure (Say, Do, Ask, Debrief, Transition) with Full 90 and Core 60 timed paths. Data rules follow Vanderbilt's approved-tool guidance and the shared traffic light: green for public or generic, yellow for internal work in approved VU tools only (ChatGPT EDU, Amplify, Copilot), red for private information about people, never.
