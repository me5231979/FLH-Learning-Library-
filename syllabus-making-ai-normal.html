Making It Normal

Modeling, norms, and leading adoption on your team

About this course

This is the 90-minute companion to the short Leading the Shift course, for managers who want the full method and the facilitator runbook. Gallup names manager modeling and workflow integration the two strongest predictors of frequent AI use on a team, ahead of tooling, licenses, and mandates, and most employees report that nobody ever told them what is actually encouraged. Workflow and Process Redesign covers the mechanics of changing how work flows; this session covers the behavior around it. The method is Show, Say, Set, Sustain: narrate your own use including the misses so that using AI imperfectly in public is safe, give explicit permission and explicit limits because silence reads as prohibition to half a team and as a free-for-all to the other half, install AI into the rituals where habits actually live, and hold the practice past the month-three novelty cliff when workload returns and usage quietly dips.

Learning objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  1. Explain why manager modeling and workflow integration predict team AI use better than tools or mandates
  2. Model AI use visibly, including the checks and the misses
  3. Give a team explicit permission and explicit limits
  4. Install AI norms into 1:1s and team meetings, with the psychological safety that makes them work
  5. Sustain adoption past the novelty cliff with protected learning time and honest measurement

Topics covered

  • The modeling effect. What actually predicts team AI use, and the permission gap sitting inside most teams
  • Show: model out loud. Narrated use, modeling the check rather than the speed, and making public fumbles safe
  • Say: permission and limits. The encouraged list, the never list, and who to ask when a case is unclear
  • Set: norms in the rituals. The 1:1 question, the share-one round, the no-blame miss log, and pairing the skeptic with the enthusiast
  • The Adoption Lab. A graded 90 days of leading a six-person team's adoption
  • Sustain past the novelty cliff. Protected learning time, celebrating caught errors, and reporting adoption as stories rather than surveillance

How you'll learn

Four scenario trainers (a research guessing game, a judge-the-model drill, a fix-the-ritual drill, and a month-three moves drill), a private permission-talk builder, the graded Adoption Lab with rerun, inline knowledge checks, a scored recap, and the capstone adoption plan. Every section pairs teaching with something to do; nothing is watch-only. Private exercises stay on the learner's screen and are never collected.

Outcomes and how learning is measured

Learners leave with a dated plan naming their team's current AI temperature, the first visible move they will make, the failure mode they are avoiding, and the ritual they are installing. Learning is measured in session through trainer scores, the graded lab, and a recap mapped to the objectives; at close through a confidence check and a spoken commitment; and afterward through a 7-day pulse and a 30-day re-poll on whether the ritual survived.

Built on Bloom's-verb objectives and instrumented with Kirkpatrick levels 1 to 3: a fist-to-five confidence check at close, a scored recap and per-section validation checks in session, and a capstone commitment card followed by a 7-day pulse and a 30-day re-poll. The facilitator edition follows an ATD runbook structure (Say, Do, Ask, Debrief, Transition) with Full 90 and Core 60 timed paths. Data rules follow Vanderbilt's approved-tool guidance and the shared traffic light: green for public or generic, yellow for internal work in approved VU tools only (ChatGPT EDU, Amplify, Copilot), red for private information about people, never.
