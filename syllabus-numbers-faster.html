About this course

Vanderbilt runs on spreadsheets, Oracle reports, and end-of-month numbers, and most of that analysis is still done by hand. Gallup's workplace research puts data and analytics among the highest-payoff AI use cases, with roughly three in four users reporting clear productivity gains and one of the widest gaps between frequent and occasional users. This session teaches the Data Loop, four moves that make AI genuinely useful on real data: describe the file before asking anything, ask in a ladder from summary to comparison to outlier, check by recomputing the numbers that matter, and tell the finding in two plain sentences. A full section is spent on getting data safely into a tool, because a spreadsheet with names in it is red-light data no matter how useful the analysis would be. Learners work a realistic month-end analysis in the graded Analysis Lab, where the failure modes are the ones that actually happen: the total that never matched the row count, the retracted number, the privacy near-miss.

Learning objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  1. Explain where AI genuinely helps with data work and where it fails
  2. Prepare a dataset safely for AI, applying the traffic light to files as well as questions
  3. Apply the Data Loop to interrogate a real spreadsheet
  4. Build a comparison and a chart brief with AI
  5. Verify AI-produced numbers before they travel to anyone else

Topics covered

  • The case. Where data work sits among AI use cases, and the specific ways AI gets numbers wrong: dropped rows, invented categories, confident bad math
  • Safe data in. The traffic light applied to files: published data, internal aggregates, and anything identifying a person, plus practical de-identification
  • Ask the sheet. Describe the columns before the first question, then the question ladder from summary to comparison to outlier to explanation
  • Check the math. Recompute one figure by hand, reconcile totals and counts, trace surprising claims back to rows
  • The Analysis Lab. A graded month-end analysis: prepare, ask, check, and tell, with outcomes at week four
  • Tell the story. The two-sentence finding, chart briefs that specify the comparison and the takeaway, and the one thing never to outsource: the recommendation

How you'll learn

Four scenario trainers (a research guessing game, a green-yellow-red file sort, a catch-the-error drill, and a judge-the-finding drill), a private data mapper that turns a learner's real dataset into a Data Loop starter, the graded Analysis Lab with rerun, inline knowledge checks, a scored recap, and the My Data Loop Card capstone. Every section pairs teaching with something to do; nothing is watch-only. Private exercises stay on the learner's screen and are never collected.

Outcomes and how learning is measured

Learners leave with a dated card naming one real dataset, the first Data Loop move they will run on it, the check ritual they commit to, and a metric (minutes to answer, compared with before). Learning is measured in session through trainer scores, the graded lab, and a recap mapped to the objectives; at close through a confidence check and a spoken commitment; and afterward through a 7-day pulse on analyses actually run and numbers actually caught.

Built on Bloom's-verb objectives and instrumented with Kirkpatrick levels 1 to 3: a fist-to-five confidence check at close, a scored recap and per-section validation checks in session, and a capstone commitment card followed by a 7-day pulse and a 30-day re-poll. The facilitator edition follows an ATD runbook structure (Say, Do, Ask, Debrief, Transition) with Full 90 and Core 60 timed paths. Data rules follow Vanderbilt's approved-tool guidance and the shared traffic light: green for public or generic, yellow for internal work in approved VU tools only (ChatGPT EDU, Amplify, Copilot), red for private information about people, never.
