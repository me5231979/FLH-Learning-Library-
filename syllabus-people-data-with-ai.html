About this course

Coaching for Performance taught the human method, and Decisions, Sharper handles judgment calls. This session teaches the people-data side, inside hard privacy lines. Culture Amp, Lattice, and 15Five are all pushing managers toward AI-assisted people insight, which makes the governing skill the ability to find patterns without ever putting a person into a tool. The spine is the Aggregate Rule: an individual is red-light data always, aggregated and de-identified team data is yellow and belongs only in approved VU tools, and published benchmarks are green. The small-team trap gets named directly, because a team-level number from a team of three identifies people. On top of that sits the Coach's Loop: aggregate, interrogate, humanize by testing every AI theme against what you actually know, and then converse, because people data that never becomes a conversation was just a dashboard.

Learning objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  1. Apply the Aggregate Rule to every people-data case, including the small-team trap
  2. Use AI to find themes in engagement results and meeting notes at team level only
  3. Draft feedback with AI as a rehearsal partner, never as a verdict
  4. Prepare a review cycle by structuring your own evidence rather than generating judgments
  5. Run the follow-up conversation the data existed to produce

Topics covered

  • The new expectation. The HR-tech wave putting AI coaches in front of managers, and the gap where survey results produce no behavior change
  • The Aggregate Rule. The traffic light applied to people data, with the small-team trap and the 1:1 notes case
  • Themes from the noise. Interrogating aggregated results, and the ground-truth test that separates a finding from a hypothesis
  • Feedback, rehearsed. AI as rehearsal partner using roles instead of names, and the line it never crosses
  • The Review Prep Lab. A graded review season for a team of eight: evidence, drafting, calibration, and delivery prep
  • From data to conversation. Sharing themes back, building the 1:1 question bank, and measuring conversations rather than dashboard views

How you'll learn

Four scenario trainers (a research guessing game, a red-yellow-green people-data sort including the small-team trap, a judge-the-draft feedback drill, and a judge-the-share-back drill), a private interrogation planner, the graded Review Prep Lab with rerun, inline knowledge checks, a scored recap, and the My People-Data Card capstone. Exercises run on the learner’s own screen and are not collected; see the privacy note.

Outcomes and how learning is measured

Learners leave with a dated card naming the people-data source they will work, the aggregation step that makes it safe, the three questions they will ask, and the conversation they have scheduled. Learning is measured in session through trainer scores, the graded lab, and a scored recap; at close through a confidence check and a spoken commitment; and afterward through a 7-day pulse on conversations actually held.

Built on Bloom's-verb objectives and instrumented with Kirkpatrick Levels 1 and 2 in session (a fist-to-five confidence check at close is Level 1; the scored recap and per-section validation checks are Level 2), with the capstone commitment card plus a 7-day pulse and a 30-day re-poll serving as Level 3 indicators; they are self-reported, so they signal transfer rather than prove it. The facilitator edition follows an ATD runbook structure (Say, Do, Ask, Debrief, Transition) with Full 90 and Core 60 timed paths. Data rules follow Vanderbilt's approved-tool guidance and the shared traffic light: green for public or generic, yellow for internal work in approved VU tools only (ChatGPT EDU, Amplify, Copilot), red for private information about people, never.
