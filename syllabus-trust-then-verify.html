About this course

Answers, Faster taught ask, anchor, check for research questions. This session teaches the broader skill as its own discipline, because AI errors do not arrive looking like errors: they arrive fluent, specific, and confident. SHRM finds workers losing meaningful hours every week to fixing AI output they trusted too quickly, which makes verification a trainable skill with a measurable payoff. The method is the Three Reads: the claim read that underlines every checkable assertion, the source read that traces the riskiest ones to something trustworthy, and the slant read that asks what the output assumes and who is missing from it. Around that sits a failure taxonomy (hallucination, staleness, slant, omission, and faked math, each with its own tell), verification budgets sized to what the output can break, and the stop rules for when a task should leave AI entirely.

Learning objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  1. Recognize how AI errors actually present themselves: fluent, specific, and wrong
  2. Apply the Three Reads to any AI output: the claim read, the source read, and the slant read
  3. Distinguish hallucination, staleness, slant, omission, and faked math by their tells
  4. Size verification to the stakes, from no check at all to expert review
  5. Decide when to stop using AI for a task altogether

Topics covered

  • The cost of belief. Why fluency fools careful people, and the weekly cleanup tax the research keeps measuring
  • Know the failure modes. The taxonomy with a one-line tell for each, practiced on real-looking outputs
  • The Three Reads. The method worked end to end on a policy summary carrying an invented deadline, a stale figure, and a slanted frame
  • Size the check. Verification budgets, the stakes ladder, and the stop rules
  • The Verification Lab. A graded hour-to-deadline briefing: what you check, the citation you cannot find, and how you sign it off
  • Make it a habit. The 90-second daily version, the no-blame miss log, and treating bias review as everyone's job

How you'll learn

Four scenario trainers (a research guessing game, a name-the-failure drill, a find-the-fault-line drill, and a check-ship-or-stop triage), a private ritual builder that turns a learner's most-trusted output into a 90-second check, the graded Verification Lab with rerun, inline knowledge checks, a scored recap, and the My Verification Card capstone. Every section pairs teaching with something to do; nothing is watch-only. Private exercises stay on the learner's screen and are never collected.

Outcomes and how learning is measured

Learners leave with a dated card naming the recurring AI output they will verify, the fault lines in it, and the 90-second ritual they commit to. Learning is measured in session through trainer scores, the graded lab, and a recap mapped to the objectives; at close through a confidence check and a spoken commitment; and afterward through a 7-day pulse asking what the checks caught.

Built on Bloom's-verb objectives and instrumented with Kirkpatrick levels 1 to 3: a fist-to-five confidence check at close, a scored recap and per-section validation checks in session, and a capstone commitment card followed by a 7-day pulse and a 30-day re-poll. The facilitator edition follows an ATD runbook structure (Say, Do, Ask, Debrief, Transition) with Full 90 and Core 60 timed paths. Data rules follow Vanderbilt's approved-tool guidance and the shared traffic light: green for public or generic, yellow for internal work in approved VU tools only (ChatGPT EDU, Amplify, Copilot), red for private information about people, never.
